About MOOD

In the MOOD for Climate Action is an experiential, learner-centred program created by the Learning Planet Institute, a United Nations University hub on the Future of Learning and a UNESCO Category II center.

We believe young people have the knowledge, creativity, and agency to address climate challenges—but they need a space to explore, learn from peers, get mentored, and build real solutions. MOOD provides that space.

Whether you choose self-paced modules or join a synchronous cohort, you'll develop systems thinking, emotional intelligence, and social entrepreneurship skills. Each module earns UN certification. The full program leads to alumni status in the Future of Learning with Youth (FLY) track.

Ikigai: Finding Your Purpose

Discover what drives you. This module guides you through a reflective journey to understand your values, strengths, and passions—and how they connect to climate action.

What You'll Do

  • Reflect on your personal values and what matters to you
  • Explore your strengths and how they align with climate work
  • Create your Ikigai diagram (purpose at the intersection of passion, skill, and global need)
  • Engage in peer dialogue about purpose and climate action

Key Outcomes

  • Clarity on your personal values and strengths
  • Articulation of why climate action matters to you
  • A foundation for choosing your climate focus area

Time & Format

~2 weeks. Self-paced, with optional reflective exercises and peer feedback sessions.

Climate Essentials

Understand climate as a system. This module teaches systems thinking applied to the climate crisis—how environmental, social, and economic systems interconnect and drive global challenges.

What You'll Do

  • Learn the science of climate change and its global impacts
  • Explore systemic connections between climate, economy, health, and social equity
  • Analyze case studies of climate impacts in different regions
  • Assess your understanding through interactive quizzes

Key Outcomes

  • Systems thinking applied to climate challenges
  • Understanding of climate-related inequality and justice
  • Ability to frame climate problems at local and global scales

Time & Format

~3 weeks. Video lessons, readings, quizzes, and peer discussion forums.

Story of Me

Craft your personal climate narrative. This module helps you articulate your relationship to climate action—your fears, hopes, and the role you want to play.

What You'll Do

  • Reflect on formative experiences that shaped your worldview
  • Write your personal climate story
  • Share and receive peer feedback in a safe space
  • Revise and refine your narrative

Key Outcomes

  • A coherent personal narrative about climate and your role
  • Emotional clarity about your commitment to climate action
  • Material for future pitches and proposals

Time & Format

~2 weeks. Reflective writing, peer feedback circles, and optional 1-on-1 coaching.

Foresight: Pathways for Change

Imagine future pathways. This module uses scenario planning to help you envision different futures and identify where your skills and passion fit into the solutions landscape.

What You'll Do

  • Explore multiple scenarios for climate futures (optimistic, pessimistic, likely)
  • Map different response strategies and actors involved
  • Identify emerging opportunities in climate action
  • Draft your personal 5- and 10-year climate goals

Key Outcomes

  • Expanded vision of possible climate futures
  • Clarity on emerging opportunities in your chosen theme
  • A personal roadmap for climate contribution

Time & Format

~2 weeks. Scenario workshops, reading materials, and group dialogue.

Project Ideation & Mentoring

Turn insights into action. This module guides you in designing a locally relevant climate project. You'll work with mentors and peers to refine your idea into a concrete project proposal.

What You'll Do

  • Brainstorm locally relevant climate challenges
  • Conduct preliminary research on your chosen problem
  • Attend mentoring sessions to refine your project concept
  • Write a project proposal with clear objectives and success measures
  • Pitch your idea to peers and gather feedback

Key Outcomes

  • A well-researched, evidence-informed project proposal
  • Mentorship connection and ongoing support
  • Readiness to move into the Build phase if desired

Time & Format

~3 weeks. Workshops, mentoring sessions, peer feedback, and project planning work.

Build & Prototype

Create real solutions. This module supports you in developing and testing an MVP (minimum viable product) for your project. Your work will be ready for potential incubation programs.

What You'll Do

  • Build an initial prototype (digital, physical, or service-based)
  • Conduct user testing and gather feedback
  • Learn social business model frameworks
  • Refine your solution based on early results
  • Prepare a pitch for external stakeholders or incubators

Key Outcomes

  • A tested, iterated prototype ready for market or scale
  • Evidence of impact and learnings from real users
  • Eligibility for social entrepreneurship incubation programs

Time & Format

~4 weeks. Hands-on building, mentoring, user testing, and iteration.
